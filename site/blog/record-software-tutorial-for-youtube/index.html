How to record a software tutorial for YouTube that viewers finish

Published October 10, 2026 · Click & Record team

To record a software tutorial for YouTube, outline the steps, size your app window so text is readable at 1080p, record with a decent mic, then edit in zooms, trim dead air, add captions and export a 16:9 MP4. The best tutorials feel fast because the creator removed every pause and zoomed in whenever the viewer needed to read something.

This guide walks through that process end to end, from the outline to upload. It works whether you teach a web app, a design tool or a developer workflow.

Plan the tutorial around one search

On YouTube, tutorials are often found through search, so start with the question a viewer would type. "How to make a pivot table in Google Sheets" is a clear video. "Google Sheets tips" is a vague one.

Then outline it:

  1. Hook (first 10 to 15 seconds): show the finished result and say what the viewer will learn.
  2. Setup: anything they need before starting, in one or two sentences.
  3. Steps: each one becomes a chapter. Aim for one action per step.
  4. Recap and next step: a quick summary and a pointer to a related video.

Write bullet points, not a full script, unless you are comfortable reading naturally. Rehearse once without recording so you know where every menu lives.

Set up your screen for readable video

Most unreadable tutorials are a setup problem, not an editing problem. Before you record:

  • Use a smaller window. A browser window around 1280 by 720 makes interface text larger relative to the frame when exported at 1080p.
  • Increase zoom in the app if text is still small. Browser zoom at 110 to 125 percent often helps.
  • Clean the stage. Hide bookmarks, close unrelated tabs, mute notifications and use a fresh demo account.
  • Prepare sample data that looks realistic but contains nothing private.

For more on sharpness, see screen recording tips for crisp video.

Record a software tutorial for YouTube, step by step

Here is the workflow in Click & Record, which is free, needs no account and adds no watermark:

  1. Pick a source. A tab for web apps, a window for desktop software, or the full display if you switch between apps.
  2. Turn on your mic and, if you want a face on screen, your webcam. The webcam is recorded separately, so you can reposition or hide it later.
  3. Start and wait for the count-in. The 3-2-1 happens before recording, so you do not have to trim it.
  4. Work through one chapter at a time. Pause between chapters to check your notes, then resume.
  5. Stop with the toolbar icon or Alt+Shift+S.

If you flub a line, pause for two seconds and say it again. A clear gap makes the mistake easy to find and cut.

Get the audio right

Viewers forgive average video far more than bad audio. Use a headset or USB mic rather than the laptop's built-in one, record in a small room with soft furnishings, and keep the mic at a consistent distance. Do a 10-second test recording and listen back on headphones before the real take.

Edit for pace: zooms, cuts and speed

Zooms

Viewers on phones cannot read a full-screen interface. Click & Record logs every click inside web pages and turns it into a zoom keyframe after recording. Each one can be retimed, made stronger or weaker, re-aimed or deleted, and you can add manual zooms, which you will need for desktop apps since an extension only sees clicks in web pages. Read how to zoom in on a screen recording for timing tips.

Cuts

Split and delete every retake, long pause and loading spinner. A tutorial that is 30 percent shorter after editing is normal.

Speed

Speed up repetitive parts, like filling a long form, to 2x or more, and leave the explanation at 1x. Per-clip speed goes from 0.5x to 4x. See how to speed up a screen recording.

Framing

A background with padding, rounded corners and a mock browser frame gives a clean, branded look. The URL bar in the frame is editable, which is handy when your demo runs on localhost.

Captions, chapters and upload

Before uploading, add the pieces that help people find and follow your video:

  • Captions. Click & Record transcribes speech locally with a built-in Whisper model, with no upload or per-minute cost. Correct product names, then export SRT and upload it in YouTube Studio. Guide: add subtitles to a screen recording for free.
  • Chapters. Add timestamps in your description, starting at 0:00, one per step from your outline. YouTube can turn these into chapters.
  • Title and thumbnail. Put the search phrase near the start of the title and show the end result in the thumbnail.

Pre-upload checklist

  • The hook shows the result within the first 15 seconds.
  • Every small UI element is readable on a phone.
  • No private data, emails or internal URLs are visible.
  • Captions are corrected and exported.
  • Chapters match the steps in your outline.

Frequently asked questions

What resolution should I record a YouTube tutorial at?

Export at 1080p in 16:9 for standard videos. Record with a browser window sized so text stays readable at that resolution, and zoom in on small UI rather than relying on viewers to go fullscreen.

Should I use MP4 or WebM for YouTube?

Both upload fine. MP4 with H.264 and AAC is the safest, most widely compatible choice, so it is a good default.

How do I add subtitles to a YouTube tutorial?

Generate captions, correct them, and export an SRT or VTT file, then upload it in YouTube Studio's subtitles section. Click & Record creates these files locally in your browser for free.

Can I turn a tutorial into a YouTube Short?

Yes. Export a short section at 9:16. Because zooms in Click & Record are editable after recording, you can tighten them so the important part fills the vertical frame.
