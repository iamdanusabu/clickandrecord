How to plan and record SaaS onboarding videos that get users to their first win

Published October 10, 2026 · Click & Record team

A SaaS onboarding video is a short, task-focused screen recording that helps a new user reach their first meaningful result in your product. The most effective approach is a small series rather than one long tour: map each video to a step on the path to activation, keep each to one to three minutes, caption it, and place it exactly where users get stuck, such as an empty state or a checklist item. Here is how to plan, record and maintain that series.

Start from activation, not from your feature list

Onboarding videos fail when they tour the interface. New users do not want to know where every menu is; they want to get one thing done. Begin by writing down your activation path: the few actions a new account needs to complete before the product becomes useful. For example, for a reporting tool:

  1. Connect a data source.
  2. Build a first chart.
  3. Share a dashboard with a teammate.

Each step is one onboarding video. If your product analytics show where new accounts stall, start with the step that loses the most people. If you do not have that data yet, ask your support team which questions new customers ask most.

Next steps: one advanced feature1 to 2 minutesAfter activation, week-1 email

Keep the welcome video personal. A founder or customer success lead on webcam saying what the user will be able to do by the end of the week sets expectations better than any feature list.

Scripting each onboarding video

Use the same three-part structure every time so users know what to expect:

  • Goal (one sentence): "In this video you'll connect your Stripe account so revenue shows up in your dashboard."
  • Steps (the bulk): Do the task at a natural pace, naming each action and what to watch out for.
  • Done state (one sentence): "You should now see last month's revenue here. Next, build your first chart."

Mention the exact labels on buttons so users can follow along, and call out anything that commonly goes wrong ("If you don't see your account, check you're signed in as an admin").

Recording onboarding videos people can follow along with

Onboarding viewers often have your product open in another window, pausing and copying what you do. That changes how you record:

  • Use a fresh demo account that looks like what a new user sees, not your power-user workspace.
  • Do not skip steps. Speed up waiting if you like, but never cut a click the user needs to make.
  • Make every click visible. The user needs to see exactly which button you pressed.
  • Type slowly when entering values the user needs to copy.

Captions and accessibility

Onboarding videos are instructions, so everyone needs to be able to follow them. Caption every video. Captions help users in open offices, users whose first language is not yours, and users who are deaf or hard of hearing. The W3C's guidance on captions is a good reference for what good captions include.

Keeping onboarding videos up to date

Onboarding videos go stale faster than any other product video, because they show the exact screens new users see. A few habits keep maintenance cheap:

  • Keep videos short and single-task so a UI change means re-recording one minute, not ten.
  • Avoid showing dates and version numbers unless they matter.
  • Keep a simple inventory: video, placement, the screens it shows, last recorded date.
  • Review after each release that touches signup, setup or the activation path.
  • Use a consistent look (same background, frame and caption style) so a re-recorded video slots in without looking out of place.

Frequently asked questions

What should a SaaS onboarding video cover?

Each video should cover one task on the path to a new user's first meaningful result, such as connecting data, inviting a teammate or creating a first project. A short welcome video can frame the series.

How long should onboarding videos be?

Keep each one to roughly one to three minutes and a single task. Short, focused videos are easier to place in context and easier to re-record when the UI changes.

Where should onboarding videos go?

Put them where the user needs them: the empty state of a feature, a welcome email, a checklist item or a help article. A video buried on a separate page is easy to miss.

Should onboarding videos have captions?

Yes. Captions help users who watch muted, non-native speakers and people with hearing loss, and they make it easier to skim. Exporting an SRT or VTT file also lets your help center player show them.
