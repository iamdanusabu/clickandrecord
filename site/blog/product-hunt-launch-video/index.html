How to make a Product Hunt launch video in a weekend

Published October 10, 2026 · Click & Record team

What a Product Hunt launch video needs to do A 75-second structure for your launch video Recording the Product Hunt launch video Editing for a crowded launch page Exporting and uploading Launch-week video checklist Make it free, fast and local

A good Product Hunt launch video is 60 to 90 seconds long, opens on the problem your product solves, shows the core workflow with zooms so the UI is readable in a small player, carries captions for people watching muted, and ends with a single line about what is free or new today. You can plan, record and export one in a weekend with a free screen recorder. Here is how.

What a Product Hunt launch video needs to do

A visitor on your launch page is skimming. They have probably looked at a dozen products already and will decide in seconds whether to upvote, comment, try or leave. Your video has three jobs:

  1. Explain the category instantly. "It's a scheduling tool for clinics" beats a clever tagline.
  2. Prove it works. Show the real product, not mockups or motion graphics alone.
  3. Give a reason to act today. A launch discount, a free tier, an open beta, a new feature.

At the time of writing, Product Hunt lets you add a video to your launch gallery, typically as a YouTube link. Check Product Hunt's current launch guide before launch day, since gallery rules and recommended sizes can change.

A 75-second structure for your launch video

TimeSectionWhat to show
0:00 to 0:08HookThe problem in one sentence over a "before" screen
0:08 to 0:15Maker intro (optional)Webcam: "I'm Sam, I built this because..."
0:15 to 0:55Core workflowThree key actions, each zoomed in
0:55 to 1:08ResultThe finished output, held long enough to read
1:08 to 1:15Launch offerWhat's free or discounted, and where to go

Do not open with a logo animation. The thumbnail and first frame already carry your brand, and every second before the product appears costs attention.

The maker moment

Product Hunt has always had a maker-focused culture, and a brief, genuine intro can make your launch feel personal. Keep it to one or two sentences. A webcam bubble in a corner while you demo often works better than a separate talking-head segment, because the product stays on screen.

Recording the Product Hunt launch video

Prepare as you would for any demo: a clean demo account with realistic data, notifications off, unrelated tabs closed, and a browser window around 1280 to 1440 pixels wide so text stays legible. Then record the whole flow in one take and cut mistakes later.

Launch videos are usually watched in a fairly small player, so zooms matter more than usual. Click & Record is a free Chrome extension that logs your clicks while you record and turns each into a zoom keyframe afterwards. The camera pushes in on what you clicked and pans between nearby clicks instead of bouncing in and out. You can retime, re-aim or delete any zoom, and add manual ones for moments that were not clicks. It also records your webcam as a separate track, so you can decide after recording whether the maker bubble stays, where it sits and how big it is.

If your product is a desktop app rather than a website, Click & Record can record that app's window too. Automatic zooms only follow clicks inside web pages, so you would add zooms manually in the editor for a native app.

Editing for a crowded launch page

  • Cut ruthlessly. Remove loading, typing and hesitation. Speed up necessary but dull steps to 2x to 4x.
  • Add a background and frame. A recording padded on a gradient with rounded corners and a mock browser frame looks deliberate next to polished competitors. The frame's URL bar is editable, so you can show your marketing domain instead of a staging URL. See adding a background and browser frame.
  • Caption it. Many visitors will watch with the sound off. Click & Record generates subtitles on your machine with a built-in Whisper model and lets you correct product names before export.
  • End on the offer. One line, on screen and spoken: "Free for makers during launch week."

Exporting and uploading

Export a 16:9 MP4 (H.264 + AAC) at 1080p for YouTube. The export dialog in Click & Record shows resolution, bitrate and estimated file size before you render, and rendering is faster than real time in supported browsers. Upload to YouTube a day or two early, set the visibility you want, add your SRT captions file to the YouTube video, and paste the link into your Product Hunt draft.

Make a few derivatives from the same project while you are at it:

  • A 9:16 cut for Shorts and Reels on launch day. See vertical screen recording for Reels, Shorts and TikTok.
  • A 1:1 or 4:5 cut for LinkedIn and X posts announcing the launch. See LinkedIn product demo video.
  • A 16:9 muted loop for your homepage. See landing page hero video.

Launch-week video checklist

  1. Video opens on the problem, not a logo.
  2. Core workflow is visible and readable in a small player.
  3. No real customer data, staging URLs or personal tabs on screen.
  4. Captions are accurate, including your product name.
  5. The launch offer is stated in the last 10 seconds.
  6. YouTube upload is live, captions attached, link tested in an incognito window.
  7. Vertical and square cuts are exported for social posts.
  8. A backup MP4 is saved locally in case you need to re-upload.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a video for a Product Hunt launch?

It is not required, but a short video lets visitors see the product working without signing up, which is exactly what most people browsing launches want. It is one of the few gallery items that can show the whole flow.

How long should a Product Hunt launch video be?

Aim for 60 to 90 seconds. Visitors are comparing many launches in one sitting, so show the core value fast and leave depth for your website.

Should the maker appear in the Product Hunt video?

A brief webcam intro or bubble from the maker often fits the Product Hunt culture, which values the people behind a product. Keep it short and let the product take most of the screen.

What format does Product Hunt need for the video?

At the time of writing, Product Hunt takes a video link, typically YouTube, in the launch gallery. Export a 16:9 MP4, upload it to your channel, and check Product Hunt's current launch guide for any changes.
