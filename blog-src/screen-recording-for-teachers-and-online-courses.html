Screen recording for teachers works best as short, single-topic videos: plan one idea per clip, record your screen with your voice and an optional webcam, add captions, and upload the file to your LMS or video host. A free browser recorder such as Click & Record covers the whole loop without an account, and keeps the video on your device until you decide where it goes.

This guide covers planning, recording, captioning and privacy for classroom lessons, flipped-learning videos and paid online courses. The advice applies whatever tool you use.

What makes a good recorded lesson

Recorded lessons fail for the same few reasons: they run long, the text on screen is unreadable, and students cannot find the part they need later. A few habits fix most of that.

  • One idea per video. "How to cite a source in APA" is a video. "Research skills" is a playlist.
  • Short by default. Many educators aim for a few minutes per clip. Treat that as a guideline, not a rule; a worked math problem may need longer.
  • Readable on a phone. Many students watch on small screens. Zoom into the part of the screen that matters.
  • Captioned. Captions help students who are deaf or hard of hearing, non-native speakers and anyone watching without sound.
  • Easy to update. Short clips mean you re-record one minute when a menu changes, not the whole lecture.

Planning a lesson before you hit record

Five minutes of planning saves twenty minutes of retakes. For each clip, write down:

  1. The objective in one sentence: what students should be able to do after watching.
  2. The steps you will show on screen, in order.
  3. The setup: which tabs or files are open, which account you are logged into, and what should be hidden.
  4. The closing line: what students should do next, such as try the practice quiz.

You do not need a word-for-word script. Bullet points keep you on track while sounding natural.

Screen recording for teachers: the recording step

Choose what to capture

Record a single browser tab for web tools like Google Docs, a quiz platform or a simulation. It keeps notifications and other tabs out of the video. Record a window for desktop software, and the full display only when you need to switch between several apps. The guide to recording a window or full screen in Chrome covers each option.

Use your face, sparingly

A webcam bubble makes a lesson feel personal, especially at the start and end. In Click & Record the webcam is recorded as a separate track, so you can move it off important content, shrink it, or hide it for a section after recording. More on that in recording screen and webcam together.

Let zoom do the pointing

Students should never squint to find the button you clicked. Click & Record logs each click and turns it into a zoom after recording, so the camera moves to the menu or cell you selected. You can adjust or delete any zoom, and add manual ones for moments where you only pointed with the mouse.

Pause instead of restarting

If a student knocks on the door or you lose your place, pause and resume. A 3-2-1 count-in before recording gives you a moment to settle, and it is not included in the file. If your browser crashes mid-lesson, the recording is saved to disk continuously and can be recovered.

Captions and accessibility

Captions are one of the most valuable things you can add to course videos, and many institutions expect them. Click & Record includes a Whisper speech model that runs inside your browser, so transcription has no upload, no API key and no per-minute cost, and it works offline. Choose the faster or the more accurate model, fix names and subject terms, and export an SRT or VTT file.

Most LMS platforms and video hosts accept one of those formats. If you are not sure which, see SRT vs VTT subtitles, and for broader guidance read making tutorial videos accessible with captions.

Privacy for classroom recordings

Teachers record screens that often contain student names, grades or messages. Treat privacy as part of the setup, not an afterthought:

  • Use a demo class or test student when showing a gradebook or LMS.
  • Close email and chat before recording, and record a single tab when possible.
  • Check the URL bar. Click & Record's mock browser frame has an editable URL bar, so you can hide an internal address.
  • Know where the file goes. Click & Record records, edits, transcribes and exports locally, with no analytics and no cookies. Nothing is uploaded unless you click Save to Google Drive.
  • Follow school policy on what may be recorded and where videos can be hosted.

If something sensitive slips into a take, hiding sensitive information in screen recordings covers crop, trim and other options.
