For most screen recordings, export MP4: it plays almost everywhere, from iPhones and PowerPoint to video editors and every major upload platform. Choose WebM when the video lives on the web, such as an embed on your own site or docs, where its efficient VP9 codec can give similar quality at a smaller file size. The MP4 vs WebM choice is mostly about where the file will be played, not about which looks better.

Below is a practical comparison, a quick decision guide, and how to switch formats without installing software.

What MP4 and WebM actually are

MP4 and WebM are containers: wrappers that hold a video stream, an audio stream and some metadata. The quality and size come mostly from the codecs inside.

  • MP4 most commonly carries H.264 video and AAC audio. This pairing has been the default for web and device video for many years.
  • WebM is an open, royalty-free format designed for the web. It carries VP8, VP9 or AV1 video and Opus or Vorbis audio.

So "MP4 vs WebM" for a screen recording usually means "H.264 + AAC vs VP9 + Opus." MDN's guide to media container formats has the full technical detail.

The simple rule: if you do not control the player, use MP4. If you do control it and it is a browser, WebM is a good option.

Serving both on the web

On your own site you can list both formats in one <video> element using multiple <source> tags. The browser picks the first one it can play, so put WebM first and MP4 second as the fallback.

Quality and file size for screen recordings

Screen recordings are unusual video: large flat areas of color, sharp text edges, and long stretches where little changes. Both H.264 and VP9 handle this well at reasonable bitrates. A few practical notes:

  • Bitrate matters more than format. A starved MP4 and a starved WebM both smear text during scrolling. Our tips for crisp screen recordings cover settings.
  • WebM can be smaller. Because VP9 is generally more efficient, a WebM export can match an MP4's look with fewer bytes. Useful for embeds and bandwidth-sensitive pages.
  • Re-encoding loses detail. Converting back and forth between formats repeatedly degrades the video. Export once from your editor, in the format you need.

Audio: AAC vs Opus

For narration over a screen recording, both AAC (in MP4) and Opus (in WebM) sound clean at typical settings. Opus is designed to handle speech well at low bitrates, which is one reason WebM files stay small. In practice, your microphone and room matter far more than the audio codec.

Resolution, frame rate and aspect ratio do not depend on format

Both formats handle 1080p, 1440p and higher, 30 or 60 fps, and any aspect ratio, including 9:16 vertical and 1:1 square. Pick those settings for the destination first, then choose the container. A 9:16 MP4 for Shorts and a 16:9 WebM for your docs can come from the same edit.

Why browser recorders often give you WebM

Browsers include a built-in recording API, and in Chrome it has long produced WebM output. That is why many extensions and web-based recorders hand you a .webm file by default. It is a perfectly good recording format, but it can cause surprises when a colleague double-clicks it and nothing opens.

One tip for teams: agree on a default. If everyone exports MP4 unless there is a specific reason not to, nobody receives a file they cannot open, and WebM becomes a deliberate choice for embeds rather than an accident of whichever tool someone used.

In short: MP4 for compatibility, WebM for web embeds and smaller files. When in doubt, MP4 is the format nobody will ever ask you to convert.
