The most reliable way to hide sensitive information in screen recordings is to keep it off screen in the first place: use a demo account, record a single tab, and silence notifications. After recording, crop out sensitive areas, cut sections where something leaked, and replace the visible URL. Blurring a specific region is a last resort and usually needs a dedicated video editor.

This guide goes through each layer of protection in order, from preparation to final review, and is honest about what each one can and cannot do.

What typically leaks in a screen recording

Most leaks are not dramatic. They are small things in the corner of the frame that nobody notices until the video is public:

  • Customer names, emails and order numbers in tables
  • API keys, tokens or session IDs in URLs, terminals or DevTools
  • Notification previews from chat or email apps
  • Other tabs, bookmarks and browser profile names
  • Internal hostnames such as staging or admin subdomains
  • Autofill suggestions that reveal your email or address
  • Revenue figures, roadmap items or calendar entries

Prepare first: the best way to hide sensitive information in screen recordings

Preparation beats editing every time. Nothing can leak if it was never captured, and you avoid trusting a blur to hold up.

Use a demo environment

Record in a demo or sandbox account seeded with realistic fake data. Names like "Acme Corp" and "jane@example.com" make the video look real without exposing anyone. If you must use production, filter tables to records you created for the purpose.

Clean up the browser

  • Close every tab you do not need.
  • Hide the bookmarks bar, or use a separate browser profile just for recording.
  • Clear or disable autofill suggestions for the fields you will type into.
  • Sign out of personal accounts in that profile.

Silence the system

Turn on Do Not Disturb or Focus mode in your operating system, and quit chat apps. A single message preview can reveal more than the whole demo.

Handle secrets deliberately

Never paste a real API key or password on camera. Use a throwaway key that you revoke right after recording, or a placeholder value.

Record only what you need

What you choose to capture matters as much as what is on screen.

SourceWhat it capturesLeak risk
Single browser tabOnly the page content of that tabLowest: no other tabs, apps or OS notifications
Application windowOne app's windowMedium: anything inside that app, including its sidebars
Entire displayEverything on the screenHighest: notifications, taskbar, other windows

For browser demos, recording one tab is the strongest default. Our guide to recording a Chrome tab walks through it. If you use a webcam, remember the camera can show your room too; in Click & Record the webcam is a separate track, so you can hide it after recording if something in the background should not be there.

Use pause as you go. If you need to open something sensitive mid-demo, pause the recording, do it, navigate back to a safe screen, and resume.

Remove sensitive information after recording

If something did slip into the take, these edits handle most cases. Click & Record's editor, also available as the free browser editor for any video file, supports all of them locally without uploading the footage.

Crop the frame

If the sensitive content sits in a fixed area, like a sidebar with account names or a status bar at the bottom, crop it out for the whole video. Cropping removes those pixels from the export entirely.

Cut the section

If a notification popped up or you briefly opened the wrong record, split the timeline before and after that moment and delete the clip. A short jump cut is far better than a leak. See how to trim and split video in the browser.

Replace the visible URL

URLs often contain internal hostnames, IDs or tokens. Add the mock browser frame and edit its URL bar to show a clean public address instead. This only covers the address bar, so pair it with a tab recording or a crop that removes your real browser toolbar. More on frames in adding a background and browser frame.

Check subtitles and audio too

If you generated captions, read them. A name you said out loud ends up in the subtitle file as text. Edit or delete the cue, and cut the audio section if needed.

When you need to blur: use another tool

To be clear: Click & Record does not include a blur, pixelate or redaction tool. If sensitive content appears in the middle of the frame and moves around, and you cannot crop or cut it, you will need a video editor that supports masking or blur, applied after you export.

If you go that route:

  • Prefer solid boxes over blur for text such as keys, emails and account numbers. Weak blur on short text can be easier to reverse than people expect.
  • Track the content. If the page scrolls, the mask must follow it in every frame.
  • Rotate exposed secrets anyway. Treat any credential that appeared on screen as compromised.

Honestly, most of the time it is quicker to re-record with a demo account than to mask a moving region.

A final review checklist

  1. Watch the full export at normal speed, then scrub through frame by frame around any risky moments.
  2. Pause on every table, form and URL and read it.
  3. Check the corners: status bars, avatars, tab titles.
  4. Read the subtitle file if you are publishing one.
  5. Have a colleague watch it with fresh eyes before it goes public.
