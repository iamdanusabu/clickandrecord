An async sales demo video is a short recorded demo, usually two to five minutes, that you send to a prospect instead of booking a first live call. To make one that gets watched, open with the prospect's name and problem on camera, show only the workflow that solves their use case, zoom in so every click is readable, and end with one clear next step. Done well, it reaches buyers who would never accept a meeting invite and gives your champion something to forward internally.

When an async sales demo video works best

A recorded demo is not a replacement for every sales conversation. It shines in specific situations:

  • Inbound leads who asked "how does it work?" but have not booked a call.
  • Follow-ups after a discovery call, showing exactly the workflow they described.
  • Multi-stakeholder deals, where your champion needs to convince a manager or a technical lead.
  • Time zone gaps where finding a live slot would take a week.
  • Specific questions such as "can it handle approvals?" that a 90-second clip answers better than an email.

For complex evaluations with procurement, security reviews and custom requirements, use async demos to support live calls, not to replace them.

Personalize the first 15 seconds

The opening is what separates an async sales demo from a generic product video on your website. In the first 15 seconds:

  1. Say their name and company on webcam: "Hi Priya, this is for the ops team at Northwind."
  2. Restate their problem in their words: "You mentioned approvals for supplier invoices are stuck in email threads."
  3. Promise a specific outcome: "In three minutes I'll show you how those approvals would work for you."

If you can, show their website, their logo in a demo workspace, or data that resembles their industry. Even small touches tell the viewer this was made for them. Use only information they shared with you or that is public, and never another customer's data.

Structure: the three-part async demo

PartLengthContent
Personal opening10 to 20 secondsName, their problem, what you will show
Tailored walkthrough90 seconds to 4 minutesTheir use case only, start to finish
Next step10 to 20 secondsOne ask: a call, a trial, or forwarding to a colleague

Resist the urge to show everything. If they asked about approvals, do not tour reporting, integrations and settings. Mention them in one sentence at the end ("there's a lot more, happy to show you reporting next") and stop.

Recording the async sales demo

Set up once, reuse often

Build one or two demo workspaces per common use case with realistic data. Then each personalized video only needs a new opening and a run through the right workflow. Close personal tabs, mute notifications and keep your CRM and inbox out of the shot. Recording just the browser tab avoids most accidental leaks.

Keep your face on screen

Make every click readable

Prospects often watch on a laptop in a small window, or on a phone between meetings. Click & Record logs each click and turns it into a zoom afterwards, so the view moves in on what you just clicked and pans between nearby clicks. You can retime or remove any zoom in the editor. It also has a 3-2-1 count-in that is not recorded, and pause and resume if you need a moment.

Talk like you would on a call

Do not read a script. Use bullet notes and speak naturally, as if the prospect were on the line. Small stumbles are fine and even make the video feel personal. Cut only the long pauses and real mistakes.

Editing: fast and light

Async sales demos are high volume, so editing should take minutes, not an hour:

  • Trim the start and end.
  • Cut any long wait or mistake with split and delete.
  • Speed up data entry to 2x or more.
  • Check the zooms land on the right things.
  • Add captions if the prospect may watch muted or is not a native English speaker. Click & Record generates them on your computer with a built-in speech model, with no upload.

A mock browser frame with an editable URL bar is handy if your demo environment has an internal-looking address.

Sending and following up

Click & Record exports MP4 or WebM on your machine and can save straight to Google Drive, where you share the file like any other Drive link. Check the sharing permissions so the prospect can open it without requesting access. Nothing is uploaded anywhere else, which helps when the demo shows information your prospect shared in confidence.

In the email:

  • Subject line: specific to them, such as "Invoice approvals for Northwind, 3-minute video".
  • Thumbnail or first line: mention the duration so they know the time cost.
  • One ask: "If this looks right, want to set up a trial for your team?"
  • Make forwarding easy: "Feel free to pass this to anyone else who handles approvals."

Follow up in two or three days if you have not heard back, referencing one specific moment in the video.

Build a library alongside personal videos

Not every prospect needs a custom recording. Keep a small library of short, generic clips (one per common question) that you can send instantly, and reserve personal videos for qualified opportunities.
