A release notes video turns your changelog into short clips that show what changed: one 15 to 30 second screen recording per notable update, optionally stitched into a monthly roundup. Users understand a new feature much faster when they see it, and a fixed, lightweight format means even a small team can keep it up every release. Here's a repeatable process for planning, recording and publishing changelog videos without turning each one into a production.

Why add video to release notes

Text changelogs are great for completeness and search, but they're easy to skim past. "Added bulk edit to the tasks table" means little until you see someone select twenty rows and change their status in one move. A short clip:

  • Shows where the feature lives, which removes a whole category of support questions.
  • Makes small improvements visible. A faster flow or a smarter default is hard to describe and easy to show.
  • Signals momentum. A regular stream of clips shows customers and prospects that the product is actively developed.
  • Gives marketing reusable content for social posts and newsletters.

This is different from a big feature announcement video. Announcements are one-off, polished, launch-day assets. Release notes videos are recurring and deliberately lightweight. If you treat every changelog clip like a launch, you'll stop making them.

A repeatable release notes video format

Consistency is what makes this sustainable. Pick a template and reuse it every time:

  1. Title card or opening line (2 to 3 seconds): the name of the change, as text on screen or a single spoken sentence.
  2. Context (3 to 5 seconds): where you are in the product.
  3. The change (10 to 20 seconds): do the thing, once, at a steady pace.
  4. Result (2 to 3 seconds): hold on the outcome.

Keep the same background, frame, aspect ratio and caption style for every clip, so they feel like a series. Many teams skip narration entirely for clips this short and rely on captions written by hand.

Before and after clips

For improvements to an existing flow, a before and after clip is the clearest format. Record the old flow on a build or environment that still has it, record the new flow with the same data, and join them with a short caption such as "Before: 6 clicks" and "After: 2 clicks." Only use numbers you actually counted. If the old version is gone, a single clip of the new flow with a caption naming what changed works fine.

Recording routine for each release

The trick is to record while the feature is fresh, not in a batch at the end of the month.

  1. When a feature ships, whoever built or tested it records a clip in a demo or staging environment with realistic data.
  2. Record one take per change. Short clips are easy to retake.
  3. Apply the template in the editor: background, frame, aspect ratio, captions.
  4. Save the export in a shared folder named by release or date.
  5. At release time, attach each clip to its changelog entry and assemble the roundup.

Building the monthly roundup

A roundup collects the month's clips into one video for your newsletter or YouTube. In Click & Record's editor, open the first clip, append the others as extra clips, trim the joins, and export once. A good roundup is a few minutes at most. Open with a ten-second list of what's included so viewers know whether to keep watching, and close with a link to the full text changelog.

Where to publish release notes videos

  • Your changelog page: embed each clip next to its entry. MP4 is the safest format for web playback.
  • In-app "What's new" panel: a muted, looping clip works well here. Captions are essential.
  • Email newsletter: link a thumbnail to the roundup.
  • Social channels: export 1:1 or 9:16 versions; the same clip can serve several platforms. See screen recording for social media marketing.
  • Internal updates: sales and support appreciate seeing changes before customers ask. This pairs well with async video updates for remote teams.

Summary

A release notes video doesn't need to be a production. Pick which changes deserve a clip, use one fixed template, record as features ship, and assemble a roundup from clips you already have.
