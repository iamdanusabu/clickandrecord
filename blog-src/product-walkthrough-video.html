A product walkthrough video is a guided tour of your product from start to finish: it follows a realistic user through the main workflow, chapter by chapter, so a prospect or new customer understands how everything fits together. It's longer and more thorough than a 90-second demo, and it works best when it's structured like a short story with clear sections. Here's how to plan, record and edit one without it turning into a meandering screen share.

When you need a product walkthrough video (and when you don't)

A short product demo video answers "why should I care?" A walkthrough answers "how does this actually work?" Those are different moments in a buyer's journey.

A walkthrough earns its length when:

  • Evaluators want depth before a trial or a sales call, such as an admin checking whether your permissions model fits their team.
  • The product has several connected parts, like data import, a dashboard and reporting, and the value is in how they connect.
  • Sales keeps giving the same tour on calls. Recording it once frees those calls for questions.
  • Customers ask "where is X?" repeatedly. A chaptered tour becomes a reference.

If the audience hasn't heard of you yet, a walkthrough is the wrong first video. Lead with a short demo and link to the walkthrough for people who want more.

Structure the walkthrough as chapters

The difference between a good walkthrough and a long screen share is structure. Before recording anything, write a chapter list.

Pick one realistic story

Choose a single user and a single goal that touches every major area. For a project management tool: "A marketing lead plans a product launch, assigns tasks, tracks progress and reports to leadership." Every chapter advances that story, which keeps viewers oriented and stops you wandering into settings nobody asked about.

A chapter template

ChapterPurposeRough length
1. OverviewWhat the product does, who it's for, what we'll build in this video30 to 45 seconds
2. Getting set upSign-in, first-run screens, importing or creating data1 to 2 minutes
3. Core workflowThe main job, done end to end with realistic data2 to 4 minutes
4. Collaboration or automationSharing, roles, integrations, rules1 to 2 minutes
5. ResultsReports, exports, the outcome of the story1 minute
6. Where to go nextTrial, docs, booking a call15 to 30 seconds

The lengths are a guideline, not a rule. If one chapter keeps growing past four or five minutes, it probably deserves its own video.

Write a beat sheet per chapter

For each chapter, list the three to six clicks or screens you'll show and one sentence you'll say at each. That's enough structure to sound prepared without reading a script word for word.

Prepare a believable demo environment

Walkthroughs show far more of the product than a short demo, so empty states and test data are more visible. Spend time here:

  • Seed realistic data for your story: plausible project names, a few weeks of activity, charts with shape.
  • Use a dedicated demo account so no real customer names or emails appear.
  • Reset state between takes, or keep a snapshot you can restore, so chapter 3 starts where chapter 2 ended.
  • Clean the browser: hide bookmarks, close other tabs, mute notifications. More in screen recording tips for crisp video.

Record the product walkthrough video in segments

Recording a ten-minute tour in one take is stressful and fragile: one stumble at minute eight means starting over. Record each chapter as a separate clip instead.

  1. Record chapter 1. Stop. Watch it back quickly.
  2. Set up the start state for chapter 2. Record it.
  3. Continue chapter by chapter. Re-record any chapter that went wrong; the others are safe.
  4. Join the clips in an editor, trimming the pauses at each boundary.

Edit for a long-form viewer

Long-form viewers forgive length but not wasted time. In the edit:

  • Cut waiting. Delete loading spinners and imports, or speed them up. Click & Record supports per-clip speed from 0.5x to 4x; see how to speed up a screen recording.
  • Add subtitles. They help viewers skim and follow along muted. Click & Record generates them on-device with a built-in Whisper model, and exports SRT or VTT if your host prefers sidecar files.
  • Keep a consistent frame. A background, padding and a browser frame make every chapter look like part of the same video. The mock frame's URL bar is editable, so you can show your marketing domain instead of a staging URL.
  • Consider your face. A webcam bubble during the overview and wrap-up adds a human touch. Click & Record stores the webcam as a separate track, so you can hide it for the detailed chapters.

Publish with chapters and a short version

Export a 16:9 MP4 for your website or YouTube. On YouTube, add chapter timestamps in the description so viewers can jump straight to the part they care about. In a help center, you can also split the export into one video per chapter.

Pair the walkthrough with a short teaser: the first 60 seconds of the core workflow, cut down, works well on a landing page with a link to the full tour. For new customers specifically, a tighter SaaS onboarding video is usually a better fit than the full walkthrough.

Summary

A product walkthrough video works when it follows one realistic story through clear chapters, uses believable data, and is recorded in segments you can retake. Edit out the waiting, add captions, and publish it with chapter markers.
