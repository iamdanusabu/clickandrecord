To record full screen in Chrome, or just a single window, use a screen recorder extension, choose "window" or "entire screen" in Chrome's share picker, select the window or display, and start recording. Unlike tab recording, this captures apps outside the browser too, so you can show a desktop tool, move between programs, or record a multi-app workflow in one take.

This guide explains when to choose window versus full screen, the setup steps, and the platform quirks that trip people up, such as macOS permissions and system audio.

Tab, window or full screen: which to choose

Chrome-based recorders typically offer three sources. Picking the right one saves editing later.

SourceCapturesBest forWatch out for
TabOne browser tab's page contentWeb app demos, website walkthroughsCannot show other tabs or apps
WindowOne application window, browser or notA single desktop app, or one browser window with several tabsPop-ups that open as separate windows may not appear
Full screenAn entire displayMulti-app workflows, OS-level settings, drag and drop between appsNotifications, desktop clutter and other private content

If everything happens in one page, tab recording is cleaner; see how to record a Chrome tab. Otherwise, read on.

How to record full screen in Chrome (or a single window)

These steps use Click & Record, a free extension for Chrome, Edge and Brave (Chromium 116+), with no account and no watermark.

  1. Install the extension and pin it to the toolbar.
  2. Arrange your desktop. Open the apps you need, close the ones you do not, and size the main window.
  3. Click the extension icon and choose window or screen as the source.
  4. Pick the target in Chrome's share dialog: a specific window, or the display you want if you have several.
  5. Enable mic and webcam if needed. The webcam is a separate track you can reposition or hide later.
  6. Record. A 3-2-1 count-in runs first and is not part of the file. Pause and resume freely.
  7. Stop with the toolbar icon or Alt+Shift+S, then edit and export.

Platform setup and common snags

macOS: grant Screen Recording permission

The first time you share a window or screen on a Mac, macOS asks for permission. If you see a black video or an empty list of windows, open System Settings, go to Privacy and Security, then Screen Recording, enable your browser, and restart it. This is an operating system requirement for any app that captures the screen.

Multiple monitors

Record the display where the action happens, and move everything you need onto it before starting. Recording a large ultrawide or 4K display produces small text when viewed on a laptop or phone, so consider recording a window instead, or plan to zoom.

System audio

Whether sound from other apps can be captured depends on your operating system and browser version. Chrome's share dialog shows an audio checkbox only where it is supported, for example when sharing an entire screen on Windows. If you need app audio, do a short test first. Narration via your microphone works everywhere.

Windows that do not appear

Minimized windows and some system dialogs may not show in the window list or may record as blank. Restore the window before starting, or switch to full screen.

Clean-desk checklist for full-screen recordings

Full-screen recordings show everything, so prepare like you would for a screen share with a client.

  • Turn on Do Not Disturb (Focus on macOS, Do not disturb on Windows) so notifications do not pop in.
  • Quit chat and email apps or at least hide their previews.
  • Tidy the desktop or use a plain wallpaper.
  • Hide the bookmarks bar and close personal tabs in the browser.
  • Use demo data rather than real customer records.
  • Increase text size in the apps you are showing, especially code editors and terminals.

If something sensitive slips through, see how to hide sensitive information in screen recordings for options.

Click zoom with window and full-screen recordings

Click & Record logs click positions and turns them into zoom keyframes after you stop, and this works for all three sources. The limitation is what an extension can see: it only detects clicks inside web pages. If your full-screen take moves between Chrome and, say, a desktop design tool, the browser clicks get automatic zooms and the desktop-app clicks do not. Add manual zooms for those in the editor, where every zoom can be retimed, re-aimed or deleted.

If you record desktop apps all day and want automatic zoom there too, a native desktop recorder may suit you better. The trade-offs are covered in Chrome extension vs desktop screen recorder.

Editing and exporting a full-screen recording

Full-screen footage often benefits from more editing than a tab capture.

  • Crop to the area that matters, such as one app on a wide monitor.
  • Trim and split out app-switching fumbles.
  • Speed up installs, builds and loading screens (0.5x to 4x per clip).
  • Add a background and padding to make a cropped window look intentional.
  • Pick the aspect ratio: 16:9, 9:16, 1:1, 4:5 or source. The export dialog shows resolution, bitrate and estimated size before you export to MP4 or WebM.

Recording a window or the full screen from Chrome gives you a free, install-light way to capture any app. Grant the permissions once, clean your desktop, choose the narrowest source that still shows everything, and the rest is the same as any other recording.
